\begin{abstract}
\noindent
$\Rn=\R\sqcup\{\nul\}$ extends the real numbers by one element $\nul$, read as
``no value was supplied'', which is declared to be the identity of both an
extended addition $\op$ and an extended multiplication $\ot$. This note states
precisely what structure results, proves the properties the software relies
on, and separates them from claims the construction does not support. In
short: $(\Rn,\op)$ and $(\Rn,\ot)$ are commutative monoids obtained by adjoining
an identity; together they are not a semiring, and the failure of
distributivity is characterised exactly. With the absorbing product $\od$
instead, $(\Rn,\op,\od)$ \emph{is} a commutative semiring, and a linear layer
that skips missing inputs is the matrix product over it. Numerically, that
layer coincides with zero imputation plus an ``everything was missing'' flag.
Measured results are reported in Section~\ref{sec:evidence}; they show no
accuracy advantage over imputation on the problems tested.
\end{abstract}

\section{Definitions}

\begin{definition}
Let $\nul\notin\R$ and $\Rn=\R\sqcup\{\nul\}$. For $a,b\in\Rn$,
\[
a\op b=\begin{cases} a+b & a,b\in\R\\ a & b=\nul\\ b & a=\nul \end{cases}
\qquad
a\ot b=\begin{cases} a\,b & a,b\in\R\\ a & b=\nul\\ b & a=\nul \end{cases}
\]
(so $\nul\op\nul=\nul\ot\nul=\nul$). Negation is $-\nul=\nul$ and
$a\ominus b=a\op(-b)$. Two further products are used for the coupling of a
weight $w$ with an input $x$:
\[
w\od x=\begin{cases} w\,x & w,x\in\R\\ \nul & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}
\qquad
w\sk x=\begin{cases} w\ot x & x\in\R\\ \nul & x=\nul \end{cases}
\]
and division is $a\oslash b=a/b$ for $a,b\in\R$, $b\neq 0$, and $\nul$ if
either operand is $\nul$. The \emph{tag} of $a$ is $\nu(a)=1$ if $a=\nul$ and
$0$ otherwise.
\end{definition}

Division of a real number by $0$ is left undefined by the algebra; the
software follows IEEE~754 there.

\section{Structure}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:monoids}
$(\Rn,\op)$ and $(\Rn,\ot)$ are commutative monoids with the same identity
$\nul$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Let $B=(\{0,1\},\wedge)$. The maps
$\varphi_+:\Rn\to(\R,+)\times B$, $x\mapsto(x,0)$, $\nul\mapsto(0,1)$, and
$\varphi_\times:\Rn\to(\R,\cdot)\times B$, $x\mapsto(x,0)$, $\nul\mapsto(1,1)$,
are injective, and a case check shows
$\varphi_+(a\op b)=\varphi_+(a)\varphi_+(b)$ and
$\varphi_\times(a\ot b)=\varphi_\times(a)\varphi_\times(b)$. Their images are
closed and contain the identities $(0,1)$ and $(1,1)$, so $\op$ and $\ot$
inherit associativity and commutativity from the product monoids.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
This is the standard construction that adjoins an identity to a semigroup
(written $S^1$ in semigroup theory, the \texttt{Option}/\texttt{Maybe} monoid
in programming). The embeddings $\varphi_+,\varphi_\times$ are exactly the
(value, mask) representation used by the code; they need different payloads
for $\nul$ ($0$ and $1$), which is why no single embedding into a product
\emph{ring} exists.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[What changes relative to $\R$]\label{prop:loss}
\begin{enumerate}
\item $0$ is no longer the additive identity of the whole set, but
      $0\op x=x$ for $x\in\R$ and $0\op\nul=0$.
\item $a\op b=\nul$ only if $a=b=\nul$. Hence no real number has an additive
      inverse, $(\Rn,\op)$ is not a group, and $x\ominus x=0\neq\nul$.
\item $0\ot a=0$ for every $a\in\Rn$: zero still annihilates. In particular a
      measured zero in a product is not ``skipped''.
\item The only invertible element of $(\Rn,\ot)$ is $\nul$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Distributivity defect]\label{prop:defect}
For $a\in\R$ and $b,c\in\Rn$,
\[
\bigl((a\ot b)\op(a\ot c)\bigr)\ominus\bigl(a\ot(b\op c)\bigr)
 = a\cdot\max\{\nu(b),\nu(c)\}.
\]
Consequently $a\ot(b\op c)=(a\ot b)\op(a\ot c)$ holds if and only if
$a=\nul$, or $a=0$, or $b,c\in\R$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
If $b,c\in\R$ this is distributivity in $\R$. If $b\in\R$, $c=\nul$: the left
term is $ab+a$ and the right term is $ab$. If $b=c=\nul$: the left term is
$a+a$ and the right term is $a\ot\nul=a$. For $a=\nul$ both sides equal
$b\op c$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:chi}
$(\Rn,\op,\ot)$ is not a semiring. The function
$\chi(x)=\bigl((1\ot 0)\op(1\ot x)\bigr)\ominus\bigl(1\ot(0\op x)\bigr)$
equals $\nu(x)$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{remark}\label{rem:chi}
$\chi$ is a consequence of the case distinctions already built into $\op$ and
$\ot$; it does not detect $\nul$ independently of them. In the (value, mask)
representation it is the mask. In floating point the identity holds for
finite $x$ only: for $x=\pm\infty$ the expression is $\infty-\infty=$ NaN.
The software therefore reads the mask and keeps the polynomial as a test.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Tag homomorphisms]\label{prop:tags}
$\nu(a\op b)=\nu(a\ot b)=\nu(a)\wedge\nu(b)$,\quad
$\nu(a\od b)=\nu(a\oslash b)=\nu(a)\vee\nu(b)$,\quad
$\nu(w\sk x)=\nu(x)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[The semiring of the linear layer]\label{prop:semiring}
$(\Rn,\op,\od)$ is a commutative semiring with zero $\nul$ and unit $1$, without
zero divisors.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$(\Rn,\od)$ is the monoid $(\R,\cdot)$ with an absorbing element adjoined, so it
is a commutative monoid with identity $1$ and $\nul\od a=\nul$. For
distributivity, $w\od(x\op y)=(w\od x)\op(w\od y)$: if $w=\nul$ both sides are
$\nul$; if $w\in\R$ and exactly one of $x,y$ is $\nul$, say $y$, both sides are
$wx$; if both are $\nul$ both sides are $\nul$; otherwise it is distributivity
in $\R$. Finally $a\od b=\nul$ forces $a=\nul$ or $b=\nul$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
This is the semiring obtained from $(\R,+,\cdot)$ by adjoining a new zero. Sums
and matrix products over it are well defined and associative, so
$y_i=\bigoplus_j W_{ij}\od x_j$ is an ordinary matrix--vector product over
$(\Rn,\op,\od)$ and such layers compose as matrices do. The ``bi-identity''
product $\ot$ plays no role in this layer; it is a separate fusion operator.
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[The directed coupling is a monoid action]\label{prop:action}
For all $w,w',x,y\in\Rn$:\;
$\nul\sk x=x$,\; $(w\ot w')\sk x=w\sk(w'\sk x)$,\;
$w\sk(x\op y)=(w\sk x)\op(w\sk y)$,\; $w\sk\nul=\nul$.
That is, $\sk$ is an action of the monoid $(\Rn,\ot)$ on the monoid $(\Rn,\op)$
by endomorphisms.
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}
A null weight under $\sk$ acts as the weight $1$. This is a pass-through, not
pruning: a pruned synapse must contribute nothing, which is what $\od$ gives.
\end{remark}

All statements of this section are checked exhaustively on a grid of
representatives, and on random arrays, by \texttt{tests/test\_algebra.py}.

\section{Relation to existing practice}

The (value, mask) pair with these rules is not new; what the project adds is a
single vocabulary across software and a tagged hardware cell.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Reductions.} $\bigoplus$ and $\bigotimes$ over an array are the
  NaN-skipping reductions \texttt{nansum}/\texttt{nanprod} with the convention
  that an all-missing reduction is missing. This is also the behaviour of
  \texttt{numpy.ma} reductions and of SQL aggregates
  (\texttt{SUM} skips \texttt{NULL} and returns \texttt{NULL} on all-\texttt{NULL}
  input).
\item \textbf{Element-wise operations.} $\od$ and $\oslash$ propagate absence
  like IEEE NaN, SQL \texttt{NULL} and \texttt{numpy.ma} binary operations.
  $\op$ and $\ot$ as \emph{binary} operations are the less common choice.
\item \textbf{Layers.} Mask-aware layers that renormalise by the number of
  valid inputs and propagate an updated mask are known from partial
  convolutions (Liu et al., ECCV 2018); missingness indicators as inputs are
  a standard baseline (e.g.\ GRU-D, Che et al., 2018).
\item \textbf{Hardware.} Skipping multiply--accumulate operations for zero or
  absent operands is established in sparse accelerators (e.g.\ Cnvlutin,
  SCNN, Eyeriss).
\end{itemize}
The word ``causal'' in the project name is historical; the algebra has no
connection with causal inference.

\section{Linear layers over \texorpdfstring{$\Rn$}{R-null}}

Let a neuron have weights $W_{i1},\dots,W_{in}\in\R$ and bias $b_i$, and let
$\tilde x$ be the input with $\nul$ replaced by $0$.

\begin{proposition}[Equivalence with zero imputation]\label{prop:impute}
If at least one $x_j\neq\nul$, then
$\bigl(\bigoplus_j W_{ij}\od x_j\bigr)\op b_i=\sum_j W_{ij}\tilde x_j+b_i$.
If all $x_j=\nul$, the sum is $\nul$ and the layer outputs $\nul$ (the bias is
not added).
\end{proposition}

So, without rescaling, the layer \emph{is} zero imputation; what it adds is
the all-missing flag and, in the implementation, the per-neuron
\emph{coverage} $k/n$ (the fraction of live synapses). Since inputs are
usually standardised, zero imputation is mean imputation.

\begin{proposition}[Rescaling]\label{prop:scale}
Let $S=\sum_j W_{ij}x_j$ and let $L$ be a uniformly random subset of
$\{1,\dots,n\}$ of size $k\geq 1$ (inputs missing completely at random), with
$S_L=\sum_{j\in L}W_{ij}x_j$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item $\mathbb{E}_L\bigl[\tfrac{n}{k}S_L\bigr]=S$: the factor $n/k$ gives an
      unbiased estimate of the full sum.
\item If the $x_j$ are independent with mean $0$ and variance $1$, then
      $\mathbb{E}\bigl[\tfrac{n}{k}S_L^2\bigr]=\mathbb{E}[S^2]$: the factor
      $\sqrt{n/k}$ preserves the second moment, whereas the factor $n/k$
      inflates it by $n/k$ and no rescaling shrinks it by $k/n$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Each index lies in $L$ with probability $k/n$, so
$\mathbb{E}_L[S_L]=\tfrac{k}{n}S$ and, under the assumptions of (2),
$\mathbb{E}[S_L^2]=\mathbb{E}_L\sum_{j\in L}W_{ij}^2=\tfrac{k}{n}\sum_jW_{ij}^2
=\tfrac{k}{n}\mathbb{E}[S^2]$.
\end{proof}

The implementation offers both (\texttt{normalize="mean"} and
\texttt{"variance"}). Neither is correct for correlated inputs in general, and
neither replaces a model of the missing values.

\section{Frozen parameters}

$W\op\nul=W$ expresses ``do not update this entry''. Giving the update of a
set of entries the value $\nul$ is a masked update,
$W\leftarrow W+\Delta W$ on the complement of the mask, and produces the same
numbers bit for bit.

For comparison, AdamW with a gradient that is merely zero applies
$W_{t+1}=(1-\eta\lambda)W_t-\eta\,\hat m_t/(\sqrt{\hat v_t}+\varepsilon)$, so
once the moment estimate has decayed
$W_t\approx(1-\eta\lambda)^tW_0$ and the mean squared drift is
$\bigl(1-(1-\eta\lambda)^t\bigr)^2\,\mathbb{E}[W_0^2]$. For $\eta=10^{-3}$,
$\lambda=2\cdot10^{-2}$, $t=500$ this is about $10^{-4}$. The drift is real but
small, and excluding the entries from the update removes it without any
algebra. Freezing parameters does not by itself address catastrophic
forgetting, which concerns which parameters to protect and how the remaining
capacity is used.

\section{The tagged multiply--accumulate cell}

\texttt{hardware/rnull\_mac.v} implements
$\mathrm{acc}\leftarrow\mathrm{acc}\op(w\od x)$ on signed $W$-bit operands, with
one tag bit per operand. On a null operand the multiplier inputs are forced to
zero and the accumulator is not enabled. The output tag \texttt{acc\_null}
distinguishes ``nothing was accumulated'' from a real sum equal to $0$; this is
the only functional difference from a conventional sparse MAC with zero
skipping. The accumulator has $2W+G$ bits; since $|wx|\leq2^{2W-2}$, any
$N\leq2^{G+1}-1$ products are summed exactly, and a sticky flag records
overflow beyond that.

If weights and inputs are null independently with probabilities $p_w,p_x$, the
fraction of skipped cycles is $1-(1-p_w)(1-p_x)$. This is an \emph{activity}
figure. No synthesis, timing, area or power results exist for this cell; the
tag bits themselves cost storage and routing.

\section{Scope}

What the construction provides: a consistent way to carry ``absent'' through
sums, products and linear layers; an explicit all-missing output instead of a
bias-only output; exact freezing of selected entries; and a hardware cell
whose result tag distinguishes an empty sum from a zero sum.

What it does not provide, and earlier versions of the project material
overstated: out-of-distribution detection (only the case of entirely missing
input is flagged); a remedy for catastrophic forgetting; measured energy
savings; or a predictive advantage over imputation.
